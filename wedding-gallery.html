What's On This Page

Ten frames, and the quiet parts in between

These are wedding photographs, not styled set pieces. An outdoor ceremony under a wooden arch dressed with greenery. Rings going on. The two of them holding hands under a flowered arch. The kiss itself, during the ceremony, while everyone is still standing. A bride with her bouquet down by a pond, the two of them again by a lake, and then the pair of them laughing back-to-back once the reception got going. That last one is the kind of frame I'm actually hunting for all day.

There's a pergola full of flowers on here too, and a dessert table of decorated cookies and roses, because someone put real work into those and they deserve a frame of their own. The rings show up twice: once going on, and once laid out on white fabric with the flowers. The details are part of the story, and they're also the part that disappears first — by the end of the night the cookies are gone and the arch is coming down.

I photograph weddings in Sacramento, Folsom, Granite Bay, Lincoln and Rocklin, and in Roseville, which is where I live. If you want to know how I work, the frames are better evidence than anything I could write here: nobody in them was told where to stand, and I was already on the ring hand before the ring went on.

After The Day

What arrives, and when

The gallery lands four to six weeks after the wedding — a private link, full-resolution files, yours to download. Every frame in it is finished work; there's no round of proofs to pick through first. Coverage runs from four hours up to a full ten-hour day, and the wedding collections page lays out what each one includes so you can work out which fits your timeline instead of guessing.

If the date is still a long way off, start with an engagement session — the collections, the lengths and what comes with each one are all laid out there.
